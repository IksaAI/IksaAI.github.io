01

Where did the space go?

Click a category and its contents appear below: how big your Photos library is, how much Ollama's models take, how large DaVinci Resolve's render cache has grown. Common space hogs are listed by name, so you don't have to dig through folder after folder. The largest files are listed too, leaving out system files and the insides of apps.

02

Is the drive OK?

HDInfo reads the health data your drive keeps about itself — temperature, how much of its life is used, available spare, power-on hours, unsafe shutdowns — and sums it up as one of four states: Healthy, Warning, Failing, Failed. It alerts you when the temperature passes the limit you set, and the menu bar shows it at a glance.

03

Clear caches — only caches

One row per app, saying what it is and what happens when you clear it. Nothing can be cleared while its app is running, and anything with a bigger impact is never checked by default.

Cleared
Browser caches · caches of apps like VS Code, Discord and Slack · download caches from Homebrew, npm and pip · DaVinci Resolve's render cache · photos, videos and files you've opened in LINE
Never touched
LINE chat history and stickers · browser bookmarks, passwords and site data · DaVinci project backups and stills galleries · the apps themselves

04

Duplicate files

Compared step by step — size, the beginning, the end, then the full contents — and only among your own files. Files in iCloud or Dropbox that aren't downloaded yet won't be downloaded just to compare them.

05

Remove apps and leftovers

Settings, caches and login items are found along with the app, and you check each one. It also finds what's left behind by apps you've already deleted.

Four things HDInfo never does

  1. It never deletes outright.Everything goes to the Trash, and you always confirm first.
  2. It never reports back.No telemetry. Your file list never leaves this Mac.
  3. It never follows shortcuts.Symbolic links aren't followed, so nothing gets cleared somewhere else.
  4. It never installs extras.Only what's built into macOS — no drivers, no background services.

06

Install

  1. Download and open the DMG

    Download HDInfo {{version}}, then open HDInfo-{{version}}.dmg.

  2. Drag HDInfo to Applications

    In the window that opens, drag the HDInfo icon onto the “Applications” folder on the right.

  3. First launch: allow it to run

    HDInfo isn't notarized by Apple yet, so macOS blocks it the first time you open it. That's expected. Do this once and it opens normally from then on.

    macOS 15 or later (Sequoia, Tahoe)
    1. Open HDInfo from Applications. When the “Apple could not verify…” dialog appears, click Done.
    2. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll down to “Security.”
    3. Next to “HDInfo was blocked…,” click Open Anyway, enter your password, then click Open Anyway again.
    macOS 14 Sonoma
    1. In Applications, right-click (or Control-click) HDInfo and choose Open.
    2. Click Open again in the dialog that appears.
    Comfortable with Terminal? One line does it
    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/HDInfo.app

  4. Allow Full Disk Access

    Space analysis and cleanup need to read the whole disk. The first time you use them, HDInfo takes you to System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access to turn on HDInfo. It works without it, but macOS will ask about each protected folder one at a time, and the numbers come out lower.
